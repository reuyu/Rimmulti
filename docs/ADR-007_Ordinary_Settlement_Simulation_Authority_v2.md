Context & BaselineThis ADR clarifies who is authorized to simulate ordinary time for a settlement and how client-produced progress becomes a candidate for canonical state.   2. Canonical Custodian vs. Leased Simulation WriterDedicated Server: Retains final Canonical Custody of the settlement graph.   Owner Client: Operates as a Leased Simulation Writer. The client may submit ordinary simulation progress only while it holds a valid WriterLeaseId.   3. Lease, Fencing, and Identity SeparationWrite authority is strictly separated from session or process identity:FencingGeneration: A monotonic, server-issued value used exclusively for canonical write fencing.   WriterLeaseId: The active lease granting submission authority, which utilizes the FencingGeneration to authorize commits.   SessionEpoch / WorkerGeneration: These serve as principal identifiers referenced by the lease, but are not conflated into the fencing generation itself.   Disconnect & Fencing: The server cannot trust a client to physically stop simulating upon disconnect; a stale client may continue locally. Canonical safety is enforced entirely by server-side lease revocation and FencingGeneration rejection, blocking any stale results. Checkpoint loss/grace policy remains OPEN (G-Q6).   4. Ordinary ↔ Tactical Handoff SequenceWhen a lawful attack or approved visit requires transitioning from an online owner to a Tactical Worker, the handoff must follow a strict, non-blocking sequence:   Freeze/Barrier: Instruct the current writer (client) to freeze simulation.   Final Checkpoint Candidate: Receive the final checkpoint candidate while the current lease is still valid.   Validate/Commit: Validate and commit the candidate to canonical state.   Revoke/Fence: Revoke the client's lease and increment the fencing generation to fence off the old writer.   Grant Worker Lease: Issue a new lease to the Tactical Worker, accompanied by a MaterializationManifest.
Fallback Rule: If the client is dead, crashed, or unresponsive, the server must not wait for a final upload. It must immediately revoke the lease, fence the client, and use the last committed canonical head to construct the Worker's materialization package.   5. Master Decisions Required (Explicitly OPEN)The following decisions require Master policy input and are not resolved by this ADR:G-Q1: Multi-Settlement Topology   Option A: Player-scoped clock (conquest reprojects L_s onto the owner's clock).   Option B: One active settlement per client (others remain frozen).   Option C: Per-map clock virtualization (validated engine design isolating clocks internally).   Option D: Separate execution islands / remote ordinary simulation (server orchestrates multiple ordinary workers).   G-Q2: Ordinary Simulation Anti-Cheat & Residual Trust Posture   Option A: Plausibility bounds & audit (heuristic delta validation, accepting residual cheat risk).   Option B: Server-hosted ordinary simulation (maximally secure, but contradicts SRS §4.2).   G-Q6: Checkpoint Loss Window & Disconnect Grace   Decision: Determines how much uncommitted ordinary progress is lost upon sudden socket drop, balancing interval requirements against network overhead.   Terminology & Cross-ADR Contract DefinitionsBoth ADR-003 and ADR-007 strictly share the following glossary to prevent overlapping authority:LocalClockId: The identity of the temporal domain (ADR-006A).   WriterLeaseId: The active lease granting a client or Worker the authority to submit state proposals.   FencingGeneration: A monotonic, server-issued value used by the lease to guarantee canonical write fencing and block stale uploads.   CommittedSettlementStateRevision: The unified integer representing the completely validated canonical state. Does not advance for queued/pending operations.   ArtifactRevision: The specific revision of the physical materialization artifact/package.   SettlementProjectionRevision: The revision of the durable server-side projection/index.   CheckpointParentRevision: The CommittedSettlementStateRevision declared by an incoming checkpoint, used by the fencing service to ensure fast-forward-only submissions.   MaterializationManifest: The payload of queued cross-boundary operations (deltas, ingress) and Faction registry data handed to a newly leased writer for application during load.   EntityId: The server-issued global identity for cross-boundary/persistent entities, mapped to native engine IDs.   Delivery Summary & Outstanding ItemsRemaining Contradictions: None.
